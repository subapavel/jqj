# F-45 — Wide constructs lower to linear IR depth, and the run recurses on it

- **Assumption**: ADR-0035 and ADR-0040 lower a construct with many parts (object pairs, operator terms, interpolation parts, pattern variables, `and`/`or` terms) to an IR whose depth grows with the number of parts. IrVerifier, Compiler and the run then recurse on that depth. F-44 bounded the built IR at 512 levels.
- **How it fails** (the #70 implementer at 1c5803e, `.logs/70-wide.log`, `70-wide-b0b5095.log`, `70-wide-run.log` in its worktree):
  - Under the bound, the first width that is a Stub is:
    - 257 terms of `.a+` or `.a*` (two Binds per operator);
    - 171 pairs of `{(.a): .a, …}`;
    - 256 pairs of `{(.a): 1, …}`;
    - 511 objects of computed values;
    - 511 interpolations;
    - 509 pattern variables;
    - 512 value parameters;
    - 512 terms of `and` or `or`.
  - At b0b5095 these compiled at 20 000 on the test thread, and jq-ref runs them. On a 512 KiB thread they were a latent overflow.
  - Even under the bound, the run overflows a fresh 512 KiB thread on 511 terms of `or` (IfOp) and on 508-variable patterns (Intrinsic1Op). b0b5095 does the same.
- **Severity**: major. An integration mapping with a few hundred computed keys is plausible (founding architecture: integration filters), and a Stub or an overflow there is a regression from jq.
- **Evidence**: the #70 hand-back at 1c5803e; IrDepthTest's 18 DEVIATIONS "IR depth" rows.
- **Change made**: none in #70. #70 keeps the F-44 bound, since a Stub is better than an overflow, and lists every width in DEVIATIONS. A new ticket lowers wide constructs with logarithmic IR depth, so that the IR is balanced or a run is flat and the run does not recurse per part. The ticket must land before the 2a spec PR. #86, the Binder and Resolver recursion on operator chains, stays its own ticket.
