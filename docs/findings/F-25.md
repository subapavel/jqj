# F-25 — jq's read block also completes a UTF-8 character cut at 4091 bytes

- **Assumption** (grilling round 2, `(at <stdin>:N)`; #34's ticket): a block is cut after an LF or
  after 4091 bytes.
- **How it fails**: jq 1.8.2's `util.c` reads with `fgets` into a 4096-byte buffer
  (`max_gets_len = 4092`). When a block ends inside a UTF-8 character, it backtracks
  (`jvp_utf8_backtrack`) and reads the missing bytes with `fread`. So the block is longer than
  4091 bytes, and every later block boundary moves.
- **Severity**: minor. It changes N only for inputs with a multi-byte character across byte 4091
  of a block.
- **Evidence**: the designer's draft for #34 (conflict C3), 2026-11-11, from reading the 1.8.2
  source. It is UNVERIFIED until #34 records `field/line-utf8-cut` from jq-ref.
- **Change made**: #34 builds the rule as the recording shows it. A read block is the bytes through
  the next LF, or 4091 bytes plus the rest of a UTF-8 character cut there (GLOSSARY *Read block*).
  The grilling rule's intent, "N follows jq-ref's rule", stands; no ADR changes.
- **Confirmed on jq-ref 1.8.2** (#34's recordings and ErrorLineCutTest):
  - the lead byte at or before the cut decides how many bytes follow: C0, C1 and F5–FF take 0,
    C2–DF take 1, E0–EF take 2, and F0–F4 take 3;
  - the walk back from the cut goes over continuation bytes, so `E2 82` at the cut takes 1 more
    byte and `C3 82 82` takes none;
  - an LF pulled in by the extension does not end the block.
