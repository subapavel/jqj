# F-68 — `java.time` stops at year 999 999 999; jq-ref's dates do not

- **Assumption**: spec 4's draft, question 4: the date builtins compute the calendar with
  `java.time`.
- **How it fails**:
  - `java.time` stops at year 999 999 999 (`Year.MAX_VALUE`, JDK 25.0.5).
  - jq-ref computes far past it. A: `[1e300,0,1]|mktime` → `67768036160140800`, and
    `67767976233532800|gmtime` → year `-2147483648`: the year wraps to `int`.
  - A `java.time` calendar cannot represent either result, so filters that jq-ref runs would
    raise.
- **Severity**: major (results that jq-ref gives would become errors).
- **Evidence**: spec 4 grilling round 1, question 4 (`docs/wayfinder/spec-4-grilling.md`).
- **Change made** (spec 4, ADR-0052):
  - The calendar is jqj's own `long` arithmetic in O(1), wrapping to `int` where jq-ref does. Being
    O(1), it needs no tick however far the year.
  - The JDK supplies zone offsets only.
  - The `gmtime`/`mktime`/`strftime` ticket's red holds both A cases, plus cases at the `int` and
    `long` edges recorded on jq-ref.
